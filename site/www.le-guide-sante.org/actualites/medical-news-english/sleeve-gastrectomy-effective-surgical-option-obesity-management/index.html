Sleeve Gastrectomy: An Effective Surgical Option for Obesity Management

PAR Dr Marius Nedelcu
27 avr. 2023
Temps de lecture
5 min

Obesity has become a pervasive global health issue, affecting millions of people worldwide and leading to a myriad of health complications [1].

Conventional obesity management techniques, such as lifestyle modifications and pharmacotherapy, often fail to yield significant long-term results for many individuals, necessitating alternative solutions.

Sleeve gastrectomy, a minimally invasive bariatric surgical procedure, has emerged as an effective and increasingly popular option for managing obesity and improving overall health [2].

This article aims to provide an in-depth examination of sleeve gastrectomy, including its advantages, effectiveness, and risks, while offering guidance on post-operative care and lifestyle modifications.

The prevalence of obesity has more than doubled in the past four decades, with nearly 650 million adults affected worldwide [3].

Obesity, defined as a body mass index (BMI) of 30 or higher, is associated with an increased risk of various health issues, such as type 2 diabetes, hypertension, and sleep apnea, as well as social and economic burdens [1].

Traditional obesity management techniques involve diet, exercise, and behavioral changes, but these approaches often yield limited success in the long term [4]. Pharmacotherapy, another conventional technique, has its own limitations due to side effects and variable effectiveness.

Sleeve gastrectomy offers an alternative solution, with a growing body of research supporting its effectiveness in achieving substantial weight loss, improving obesity-related comorbidities, and enhancing quality of life [2].

This procedure involves the removal of a large portion of the stomach, leaving a smaller, tubular-shaped stomach, which helps limit food intake and promotes satiety [5]. Compared to other bariatric surgeries, such as gastric bypass and adjustable gastric banding, sleeve gastrectomy has been shown to have reduced complication rates and a more favorable impact on gastrointestinal function [5].

This article will delve into the specifics of sleeve gastrectomy, discuss its effectiveness in managing obesity, and address the potential risks and complications associated with the procedure. Additionally, we will offer guidance on post-operative care and lifestyle modifications that are essential for long-term success. For those considering surgical intervention for obesity management, sleeve gastrectomy may be a viable and effective option worth exploring with a medical professional.

Understanding Obesity

 

Obesity is a complex, multifactorial condition characterized by excessive body fat accumulation, which can have detrimental effects on health [1].

It is typically measured using the body mass index (BMI), which is calculated by dividing an individual's weight in kilograms by the square of their height in meters.

Obesity is classified into three categories: Class I (BMI of 30.0-34.9), Class II (BMI of 35.0-39.9), and Class III (BMI of 40 or higher), with Class III often referred to as "morbid" or "severe" obesity [1].

The global prevalence of obesity has increased dramatically in recent decades, with nearly 650 million adults affected worldwide [3]. This surge in obesity rates is due to a combination of factors, including genetic predisposition, environmental influences, and lifestyle choices [1].

Societal changes, such as increased consumption of high-calorie, nutrient-poor foods, and decreased physical activity, have contributed to the obesity epidemic. Obesity poses significant health risks, as it is associated with numerous medical complications.

Some of the most common obesity-related comorbidities include type 2 diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, cardiovascular disease, and certain types of cancer [1]. Moreover, obesity has been linked to sleep disorders, such as obstructive sleep apnea, and musculoskeletal issues, such as osteoarthritis [1].

These health complications not only affect the individual's quality of life but also increase the risk of premature mortality. In addition to its impact on physical health, obesity can have psychological consequences. Individuals with obesity may experience depression, anxiety, low self-esteem, and social stigma, further exacerbating their health challenges [1].

These psychological factors can contribute to a vicious cycle, where emotional distress leads to overeating or inactivity, which in turn worsens obesity and its associated health risks. Obesity also carries significant economic and social implications.

The direct and indirect costs of obesity-related healthcare, including medical expenses and loss of productivity, place a considerable burden on both individuals and society [1].

Furthermore, obesity can contribute to disparities in income, education, and employment opportunities, as individuals with obesity often face discrimination and bias in various aspects of their lives [1]. Despite the numerous challenges posed by obesity, it is important to recognize that it is a manageable condition.

Conventional obesity management techniques focus on lifestyle modifications, such as adopting a healthier diet, engaging in regular physical activity, and making behavioral changes to support weight loss and maintenance [4].

However, these approaches often yield limited long-term success, particularly for individuals with severe obesity [4]. For this reason, alternative treatment options, such as pharmacotherapy and bariatric surgery, have been developed to address obesity and its related health complications more effectively.

Among the various surgical options available for obesity management, sleeve gastrectomy has gained prominence due to its effectiveness in achieving significant weight loss and improving obesity-related comorbidities [2].

As a minimally invasive procedure, it offers advantages over other bariatric surgeries, such as reduced complication rates and preservation of gastrointestinal function [5].

Conventional Obesity Management Techniques

Conventional obesity management techniques primarily focus on lifestyle modifications, which include changes in diet, exercise, and behavior.

Despite their potential for achieving weight loss and improving health, these methods often yield limited long-term success for individuals with severe obesity [4].

Lifestyle modifications

  • Diet: A balanced, nutrient-dense diet is essential for weight management and overall health. Conventional weight loss plans typically involve reducing caloric intake and incorporating more fruits, vegetables, whole grains, lean proteins, and healthy fats while limiting the consumption of processed foods, added sugars, and unhealthy fats [4]. Portion control and mindful eating practices can also support weight loss efforts.
  • Exercise: Regular physical activity is crucial for maintaining a healthy weight and reducing the risk of obesity-related health complications. The American College of Sports Medicine recommends at least 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise or 75 minutes of vigorous-intensity exercise per week, along with strength training exercises for all major muscle groups at least twice a week [6].
  • Behavioral changes: Long-term weight loss success requires adopting sustainable lifestyle changes, which often involve addressing the underlying psychological and emotional factors that contribute to overeating and inactivity. Techniques such as goal setting, self-monitoring, stress management, and cognitive-behavioral therapy can help individuals develop healthier habits and coping strategies [4].

Pharmacotherapy

For individuals who struggle to achieve meaningful weight loss through lifestyle modifications alone, pharmacotherapy may be considered as an adjunct treatment.

Weight loss medications, such as orlistat, lorcaserin, and phentermine/topiramate, have been approved for the treatment of obesity and can help promote weight loss by suppressing appetite, increasing feelings of fullness, or inhibiting the absorption of dietary fat [7].

However, the effectiveness of these medications varies among individuals, and their use may be limited by side effects or contraindications.

Limitations of conventional techniques

Although lifestyle modifications and pharmacotherapy can be effective for some individuals, they often fail to produce significant long-term weight loss, particularly for those with severe obesity [4].

Factors such as genetic predisposition, metabolic adaptations, and environmental influences can make weight loss and maintenance challenging, necessitating alternative treatment options for obesity management [8].

Sleeve gastrectomy has emerged as a promising surgical option for individuals with obesity who have not achieved adequate weight loss through conventional techniques.

The procedure offers significant advantages over other bariatric surgeries, such as reduced complication rates and preservation of gastrointestinal function, making it an increasingly popular choice for obesity management [5].

Sleeve Gastrectomy as a Surgical Option

Sleeve gastrectomy is a minimally invasive bariatric surgical procedure that has gained popularity as an effective option for managing obesity and its associated health complications [2].

The procedure involves the removal of a large portion of the stomach, leaving a smaller, tubular-shaped stomach that can hold significantly less food [5].

This reduction in stomach size helps limit food intake and promotes feelings of fullness, ultimately leading to weight loss.

Procedure Overview

During a sleeve gastrectomy, approximately 75-80% of the stomach is removed laparoscopically [5]. The remaining portion of the stomach is fashioned into a narrow, banana-shaped pouch, which limits the amount of food that can be consumed and reduces the production of ghrelin, a hormone responsible for stimulating hunger [9]. The procedure does not involve any rerouting of the intestines, which preserves the normal absorption of nutrients [5].

Effectiveness in Weight Loss

Sleeve gastrectomy has been shown to be highly effective in achieving significant weight loss. On average, patients can expect to lose approximately 60-70% of their excess weight within the first two years following surgery [5]. Long-term studies have reported sustained weight loss of around 50-60% of excess weight at five years post-surgery [2].

Improvement of Obesity-Related Comorbidities

Sleeve gastrectomy is not only effective in promoting weight loss but also in improving obesity-related health complications. Numerous studies have demonstrated significant improvements in comorbid conditions such as type 2 diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and obstructive sleep apnea following sleeve gastrectomy [10]. In many cases, patients experience a complete resolution or significant improvement of these conditions, reducing their need for medication and lowering their risk of long-term health complications.

Enhanced Quality of Life

The substantial weight loss and improvement in comorbidities following sleeve gastrectomy can lead to significant enhancements in the patient's quality of life. Patients often report increased energy levels, better mobility, and improved self-esteem after undergoing the procedure [10]. These improvements can positively impact various aspects of life, including social interactions, employment opportunities, and overall psychological well-being.

Indications and Patient Selection

Sleeve gastrectomy is typically recommended for patients with a BMI of 40 or higher, or a BMI of 35 or higher with at least one obesity-related comorbidity, such as type 2 diabetes or hypertension [5]. In some cases, sleeve gastrectomy may also be considered for individuals with a BMI between 30 and 34.9 who have not achieved adequate weight loss through conventional methods and have obesity-related health complications [5].

Patient selection should also consider factors such as the patient's age, overall health, and willingness to commit to long-term lifestyle changes. In the subsequent sections, we will discuss the potential risks and complications of sleeve gastrectomy, provide guidance on post-operative care and lifestyle modifications essential for long-term success, and compare sleeve gastrectomy to other bariatric surgery options.

Effectiveness of Sleeve Gastrectomy

 

The effectiveness of sleeve gastrectomy as a surgical option for obesity management can be assessed by examining its impact on weight loss, improvement in obesity-related comorbidities, and enhancement in the quality of life.

Weight Loss Outcomes

Sleeve gastrectomy has demonstrated significant weight loss results in both the short and long term. Studies have shown that patients can expect to lose approximately 60-70% of their excess weight within the first two years following surgery [5]. Long-term follow-up data has reported sustained weight loss of around 50-60% of excess weight at five years post-surgery [2]. These outcomes highlight the effectiveness of sleeve gastrectomy in promoting substantial and lasting weight loss for individuals struggling with obesity.

Improvement in Obesity-Related Comorbidities

The impact of sleeve gastrectomy extends beyond weight loss, with significant improvements observed in obesity-related health complications. Research has shown that many patients experience a complete resolution or considerable improvement in comorbid conditions such as type 2 diabetes, hypertension, dyslipidemia, and obstructive sleep apnea following the procedure [10]. In some cases, the improvements in these conditions have allowed patients to reduce or even discontinue their medications, lowering their risk of long-term health complications and enhancing their overall well-being [5].

Enhanced Quality of Life

Patients who undergo sleeve gastrectomy often experience significant improvements in their quality of life. The substantial weight loss and alleviation of comorbidities lead to increased energy levels, improved mobility, and enhanced self-esteem [10]. These positive outcomes can impact various aspects of a patient's life, including social interactions, employment opportunities, and overall psychological well-being. Furthermore, the improved health status of patients can contribute to a reduction in healthcare costs and resource utilization associated with obesity and its related complications [4].

Long-Term Success and Patient Satisfaction

The long-term success of sleeve gastrectomy is highly dependent on patients' adherence to post-operative guidelines and lifestyle modifications, such as dietary changes, regular physical activity, and ongoing support from healthcare providers [5]. Studies have reported high levels of patient satisfaction following sleeve gastrectomy, with many patients expressing an improved quality of life and the ability to maintain their weight loss long-term [8]. This high level of satisfaction further underscores the effectiveness of sleeve gastrectomy as an obesity management solution.

Comparison to Other Bariatric Procedures

When comparing the effectiveness of sleeve gastrectomy to other bariatric procedures such as Roux-en-Y gastric bypass (RYGB) and adjustable gastric banding (AGB), sleeve gastrectomy has been shown to achieve comparable or superior weight loss outcomes and improvements in comorbidities [2, 3]. 

Additionally, sleeve gastrectomy is generally associated with fewer complications and a less complex surgical procedure compared to RYGB, making it a more attractive option for many patients and healthcare providers [5].

In summary, sleeve gastrectomy has proven to be a highly effective surgical option for obesity management, offering significant weight loss outcomes, improvements in obesity-related comorbidities, and enhancements in the quality of life for patients who have struggled with conventional weight loss methods.

Risks and Complications

As with any surgical procedure, sleeve gastrectomy carries inherent risks and potential complications.

It is crucial for patients and healthcare providers to be aware of these risks and weigh them against the potential benefits when considering this surgical option for obesity management.

Short-term Complications Bleeding: Postoperative bleeding may occur at the site of stomach stapling or the incisions made during surgery. Although the risk of bleeding is relatively low, it may require blood transfusions or additional surgical intervention in some cases [11].

Infection: Infections can develop at the incision sites or within the abdominal cavity. These complications may necessitate the use of antibiotics or further surgical intervention [11].

Leaks: Leaks can occur along the staple line, leading to the release of stomach contents into the abdominal cavity. This complication can cause infection or abscess formation and may require reoperation to address the issue [11].

Blood clots: The formation of blood clots in the legs or lungs is a potential risk following any major surgery, including sleeve gastrectomy. To minimize this risk, patients may receive blood thinners and be encouraged to mobilize as soon as possible after surgery [12].

Long-term Complications Nutrient deficiencies: Although sleeve gastrectomy does not cause malabsorption, the reduced food intake may lead to deficiencies in certain nutrients, such as vitamin B12, iron, and calcium. This issue necessitates supplementation and regular monitoring of nutritional status [11].

Gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD): Some patients may experience an increase in GERD symptoms following sleeve gastrectomy due to changes in stomach anatomy. In severe cases, this may require the use of medications or additional surgical intervention [11].

Weight regain: While sleeve gastrectomy typically results in significant weight loss, some patients may regain a portion of the lost weight over time. Long-term success is dependent on patients' adherence to postoperative dietary and lifestyle recommendations [5].

Stenosis or stricture: A narrowing of the sleeve may occur in some patients, causing difficulty swallowing or blockages. Treatment may involve the use of endoscopic dilation or, in rare cases, surgical revision [13].

Minimizing Risks and Complications

To minimize the risks and complications associated with sleeve gastrectomy, it is essential for patients to: Choose an experienced surgeon: Selecting a surgeon with extensive experience in performing sleeve gastrectomy procedures can help reduce the risk of complications [11].

Comply with preoperative recommendations: Patients should follow all preoperative guidelines provided by their healthcare team, such as losing weight, quitting smoking, and managing medical conditions [12].

Adhere to postoperative care and lifestyle modifications: Following postoperative care instructions and adopting recommended dietary and lifestyle changes can help minimize complications and maximize long-term success [5].

Despite the potential risks and complications, sleeve gastrectomy remains an effective and relatively safe option for obesity management. By carefully considering these factors and working closely with their healthcare team, patients can maximize the benefits and minimize the risks associated with this surgical procedure.

Post-operative Care and Lifestyle Modifications

To maximize the success of sleeve gastrectomy and maintain long-term weight loss, patients must commit to post-operative care and lifestyle modifications.

These changes involve diet, exercise, and emotional support, all of which play a crucial role in ensuring a healthy and sustainable post-surgery life.

Dietary Changes

Gradual reintroduction of food: After surgery, patients will need to follow a specific dietary plan provided by their healthcare team, which typically involves a gradual progression from clear liquids to pureed foods, then soft foods, and finally solid foods over several weeks [14].

Smaller, more frequent meals: Due to the reduced stomach size, patients will need to consume smaller meal portions and eat more frequently throughout the day. This change helps avoid overeating and ensures adequate nutrient intake [14].

Balanced and nutrient-dense foods: Patients should prioritize consuming a balanced diet rich in lean proteins, fruits, vegetables, and whole grains while avoiding high-calorie, high-fat, and high-sugar foods [12].

Nutritional supplements: To prevent nutrient deficiencies, patients may be advised to take specific vitamin and mineral supplements, including vitamin B12, iron, and calcium, as recommended by their healthcare provider [11].

Exercise and Physical Activity Gradual increase in activity: Following surgery, patients should gradually increase their physical activity levels as directed by their healthcare team [12]. Early mobilization helps prevent blood clots and accelerates the recovery process.

Regular exercise: Engaging in regular exercise, such as walking, swimming, or resistance training, can help patients maintain weight loss and improve overall fitness [12]. Aim for a minimum of 150 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic activity per week, along with muscle-strengthening activities on two or more days a week [15].

Personalized exercise plan: Patients should work with their healthcare team to develop an individualized exercise plan that aligns with their abilities and preferences [15].

Emotional Support and Mental Health Support groups: Participation in support groups, either in-person or online, can provide emotional encouragement and practical advice for patients adjusting to post-surgery life [16].

Counseling or therapy: Some patients may benefit from counseling or therapy to address emotional eating, body image concerns, or other mental health issues related to their weight loss journey [16].

Ongoing communication with healthcare providers: Maintaining regular communication with the healthcare team can help patients address any concerns or challenges they encounter throughout their post-operative journey [5].

Monitoring and Follow-up Regular follow-up appointments: Patients should attend all scheduled follow-up appointments with their healthcare team to monitor weight loss progress, nutritional status, and overall health [12].

Adjusting the plan as needed: The healthcare team may need to adjust the patient's care plan over time to address any ongoing issues, such as weight regain or nutrient deficiencies [5].

By adhering to these post-operative care guidelines and embracing lifestyle modifications, patients can optimize their weight loss success and enhance their overall health and well-being following sleeve gastrectomy.

Conclusion

Sleeve gastrectomy has proven to be an effective surgical option for obesity management, offering significant weight loss and improvements in obesity-related comorbidities [5].

As a minimally invasive procedure, it has gained popularity due to its lower risk of complications compared to other bariatric procedures, such as gastric bypass and adjustable gastric banding [11].

However, the long-term success of sleeve gastrectomy relies heavily on patients' commitment to post-operative care and lifestyle modifications. Adherence to dietary guidelines, regular exercise, emotional support, and ongoing communication with healthcare providers are crucial components in achieving lasting weight loss and improved overall health [12, 14, 15, 16].

While sleeve gastrectomy is not a one-size-fits-all solution for obesity, it provides a viable option for individuals who have not achieved success through conventional weight management approaches.

With proper patient selection, preparation, and ongoing support, sleeve gastrectomy can be a life-changing intervention for those seeking a healthier and more fulfilling future.
